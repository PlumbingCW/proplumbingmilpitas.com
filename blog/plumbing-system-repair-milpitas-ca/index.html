Plumbing System Repair: What to Fix First in a Milpitas, CA Home

A home plumbing system is a few separate systems working together: the water supply, the drain lines, the heating and the fixtures. When something fails, it helps to know which system it belongs to, because that tells you what to do first. This guide sorts the common repairs by urgency and by who should do them.

  • Written for Milpitas homes.
  • Plain steps.

Four systems, four sets of problems

The water supply system brings clean water in under pressure. Its problems are leaks, low pressure and burst pipes. The drain system carries waste away by gravity. Its problems are clogs, slow drains and backups. The hot water system heats the water. Its problems are no hot water, leaks and noise. The fixtures are where you touch the system. Their problems are drips, running toilets and weak flow.

Naming the system helps you pick the right service. A pressure problem is not a drain problem, and a water heater leak is not a pipe leak. When you call, describe the symptom and what you have already tried.

Pressure is the quiet cause of many repairs

High water pressure wears out fixtures, valves and the water heater. The California Plumbing Code material the City of Milpitas distributes calls for a pressure regulator when pressure exceeds 80 psi. A gauge on a hose bib tells you the number in a minute. If it reads above 80, the regulator needs attention, and fixing it prevents more repairs.

Low pressure has different causes. A partly closed valve, a clogged aerator, a failing service line or corroded galvanized pipe can all lower flow. Test at an outdoor spigot first. If pressure there is fine and a fixture is weak, the problem is local to that fixture. If pressure is low everywhere, it points to the supply side. See water line repair.

Repairs a homeowner can reasonably do

  • Replace a toilet flapper or fill valveParts are inexpensive and instructions are on the package.
  • Clean an aeratorUnscrew it, rinse out minerals and reattach.
  • Replace a faucet cartridgeTurn off the supply first.
  • Replace a supply line under a sinkTurn the stop valve off first and use braided lines.
  • Clear a simple sink clog with a plungerUse a cup plunger and a wet cloth to seal other openings.
  • Flush the water heaterOnce a year, following the manual.

Repairs better left to a plumber

  • Anything involving the main water line or sewerMistakes are costly and permit rules apply.
  • Water heater replacementGas, venting and strapping must be done to code. See water heater replacement.
  • Pipe replacement inside wallsIt needs a permit and inspection.
  • Repeat clogsA camera finds the cause. See sewer camera inspection.
  • Slab leaksNeed detection tools. See slab leak repair.
  • Gas workOnly a qualified plumber should touch gas piping.

Local note

What the City of Milpitas says is exempt from a permit

The city's Building Safety FAQ says that repairs which do not replace or rearrange pipes do not need a plumbing permit. Fixing a leaking joint, swapping a faucet cartridge or clearing a drain falls under that. Replacing a section of pipe, a water heater or a fixture connection that changes the layout is different and needs a permit.

Knowing the line helps you plan. A small repair done today is permit-free. A larger project later needs a permit and an inspection. We tell you which side of the line a job falls on before work starts. If you live in North San Jose, our San Jose service page covers the City of San Jose permit rules, which are separate.

Source type: City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety FAQ and permit handout.

A repair routine that saves money

  1. Learn your shutoffs

    Find the main water valve and each fixture stop valve.

  2. Check pressure once a year

    A cheap gauge on a hose bib is enough.

  3. Look under sinks every few months

    Catch drips before they rot the cabinet.

  4. Flush the water heater yearly

    It reduces sediment and extends tank life.

  5. Photograph repairs and keep receipts

    It helps with resale and insurance.

Keep a plumbing file for the house

A folder with receipts, permit numbers, the water heater label and photos of the shutoff valves makes every future repair easier. A plumber who knows what was done before can skip guesswork. A buyer, an insurer or the city may also ask for records of major work.

Add the date and the name of whoever did the work. Add a photo of the pipe before walls are closed up, because it is the only record of what is inside the wall once the drywall goes back on. Five minutes at the time of a repair saves hours later.

When a repair is really a replacement

If you keep fixing the same fixture, the money spent adds up. A toilet that clogs monthly, a faucet whose parts are no longer sold and a water heater with its third repair are all candidates for replacement. A good plumber will say so when a repair is not the best use of your money.
