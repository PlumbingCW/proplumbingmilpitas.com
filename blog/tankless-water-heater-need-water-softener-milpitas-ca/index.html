Does a Tankless Water Heater Need a Water Softener? Milpitas, CA Guide

A tankless water heater heats water in a compact heat exchanger. Hard water leaves scale on that exchanger, which can cut efficiency and shorten the unit's life. The question many buyers ask is whether a softener is a must. The honest answer is that it depends on your water, your use and how much maintenance you will do. This guide helps you decide.

Why scale matters more in a tankless heater

In a tank, minerals settle at the bottom as sediment. In a tankless unit, water flows through narrow channels in the heat exchanger. Minerals coat the walls of those channels as the water heats. A thin layer insulates the metal, so the unit works harder. A thick layer restricts flow and can trigger error codes or cause overheating.

Manufacturers often state in their warranties that damage from scale is not covered, and some require a softener above a certain hardness. Read the warranty for your model before you buy. The hardness limit is usually stated in grains per gallon or ppm.

Hardness scale in plain terms

Hardness (ppm)Grains per gallonWhat it means for a tankless heater
0 to 60Up to about 3.5Soft to slightly hard. Scale is slow, annual descaling is usually enough.
61 to 120About 3.5 to 7Moderately hard. Plan on annual descaling and consider a scale filter.
121 to 180About 7 to 10.5Hard. A softener is advised.
Over 180Over 10.5Very hard. A softener is strongly advised.

One grain per gallon equals about 17.1 ppm. Check your heater warranty for its limit.

What your options are

A salt-based softener removes hardness minerals and protects the whole house, including the heater, appliances and fixtures. A scale-prevention system, which uses a template-assisted crystallization or a similar media, keeps minerals from sticking without removing them. It requires no salt and no drain, but it does not protect against all scale types.

A third path is to accept some scale and descale the heater every year. A service flush with a descaling solution takes about an hour and removes buildup. Many Milpitas households do this and skip the softener. The cost of a softener has to be weighed against the cost of annual service and the risk of skipped maintenance.

Local note

What Milpitas hardness means for this decision

The City of Milpitas water quality reports show a hardness range of about 8.4 to 168 ppm, with averages near 57 to 110 ppm depending on the supply and the season. At the average, hardness is in the soft to moderately hard range, which is on the low side for a tankless heater. At the top of the range, the water is hard enough that scale builds faster.

Because the mix changes through the year, a one-time test at your tap gives the best guide. If your tests are consistently under about 120 ppm, an annual descaling and a scale filter will usually protect a tankless unit. Above that, a softener is worth considering.

Signs of scale in a tankless heater

  • Error codes about flow or temperatureScale restricts flow and triggers sensors.
  • Lukewarm water at full flowThe exchanger cannot transfer heat well.
  • Noise from the unitPopping or rumbling can mean buildup.
  • Longer wait for hot waterReduced flow slows the response.
  • Visible white crust on fittingsA sign the water is leaving minerals behind.
  • Higher gas billsThe unit burns more fuel for the same heat.

How descaling is done

Most tankless heaters have two service valves on the water side. A technician closes the house supply, connects a small pump and two hoses, and circulates a mild descaling solution through the heat exchanger for about 45 minutes. The unit is then flushed with clean water, the inlet screen is cleaned and the valves are returned to normal.

Do it yearly in harder water and every other year if your readings stay on the soft side. Skipping it for several years lets scale harden, and a heavily scaled unit can be tough to clear. If your unit has never been serviced, start now rather than waiting for an error code.

Softener trade-offs

A salt-based softener adds sodium to the water in small amounts, needs salt refills and uses water to regenerate. Some cities and districts limit softener discharge, so check local rules before installing. If anyone in your household is on a low-sodium diet, ask a doctor and consider leaving a kitchen tap unsoftened.
