Reverse Osmosis Water Filtration System Cost: Milpitas, CA Guide

Reverse osmosis, or RO, pushes water through a very fine membrane that holds back dissolved solids. It is the most thorough point-of-use treatment for drinking water. It is also a system with several cost layers, and the sticker price is only the first. This guide breaks down what you pay for and how to judge a quote without any guesswork.

How an RO system works

Water passes first through a sediment filter and a carbon filter, which protect the membrane and take out chlorine. It then flows through the RO membrane, which rejects most dissolved minerals and many contaminants. The clean water goes to a small storage tank, and a dedicated faucet at the sink delivers it. The rejected water carries the concentrated material to the drain.

This is a point-of-use system, which means it treats one tap, usually the kitchen. A whole-house RO system exists, but it is large and expensive and rarely makes sense for a home. Most households do better with an under-sink unit for drinking and cooking water, and a different system for the whole house. See water softener and filtration.

What goes into the cost

We do not publish a number because the final cost depends on your choices. These are the factors that move it.

Number of stagesBasic units have three or four stages. Units with remineralization or UV add stages and cost.
Membrane and tank sizeLarger tanks hold more water. Tankless designs are compact but cost more.
Install complexityA sink with a clear cabinet is simple. Stone countertops that need a hole drilled add work.
Drain connectionThe system needs an air gap faucet or a drain saddle. Some sinks need a plumber to make it.
ExtrasA line to the refrigerator, a second faucet or a booster pump adds parts and time.
Ongoing filters and membraneCartridges are replaced every 6 to 12 months and the membrane every few years.

Is RO worth it for Milpitas water

City water in Milpitas meets state drinking water standards. RO is not required for safety, and many homeowners choose it for taste or because they want an extra layer of reduction. If your concern is a specific contaminant, a lab test of your own tap tells you whether RO is the right tool or whether a simpler filter will do.

If your main complaint is white scale on fixtures, RO at one tap will not solve it. Scale is a whole-house problem. A softener or a scale filter treats the whole house. Pairing a softener with an RO unit is common because the softener protects the membrane from hardness.

Local note

What the local water report tells you

The City of Milpitas publishes an annual water quality report listing what is found in the supply. It shows hardness that ranges from about 8.4 to 168 ppm, with averages around 57 to 110 ppm depending on the supply. Moderate hardness shortens the life of an RO membrane if it is not pre-treated, so the carbon and sediment stages matter.

Waste water from an RO unit goes to the drain. Because Milpitas homes are on the city sewer, that discharge is small and routine. Your unit should connect through a proper air gap so wastewater cannot back up into the filter.

How to compare quotes fairly

  • Same number of stagesA four-stage unit and a six-stage unit are not the same product.
  • Same certificationLook for NSF/ANSI 58 for RO units.
  • Same tank sizeTank size changes how much water you have at once.
  • Install includedAsk whether the drain connection and faucet are included.
  • Replacement parts pricedGet a list with the first-year service cost.
  • Warranty termsKnow what is covered and for how long.

Hidden costs people forget

Space under the sink is the first one. A tank, a manifold and a drain line take room that a garbage disposal and a cleaning-supply shelf may already be using. Check the cabinet before you buy. The second is the extra faucet, which needs a hole in the sink or countertop.

The third is water pressure. RO units work best with steady pressure, and a house with weak pressure may need a small booster pump. We check the pressure when we visit, so the quote does not change after the unit is on the floor.

A sensible way to decide

Start with the goal. If you want better tasting drinking water, a good carbon filter at the sink may be enough and costs less. If you want reduction of dissolved solids, RO is the tool. If you have scale problems, treat the whole house first.
