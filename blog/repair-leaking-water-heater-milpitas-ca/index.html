Leaking Water Heater: Repair Options for Milpitas, CA Homes

Water around a water heater always deserves attention, but not every leak means the tank has failed. Some leaks come from a loose fitting, a dripping valve or a condensation problem, and those can be repaired. Others come from the tank itself and mean replacement. This guide helps you tell them apart.

If water is on the floor now

Take these steps first.

Turn off the fuel or power

Switch the breaker off for electric heaters. For gas, turn the control to off or pilot.

Close the cold water valve

It is on the pipe above the heater. Turn it clockwise.

Keep people away from wet electrical areas

Do not stand in water near an electric heater or panel.

Call a plumber

A leaking tank can empty 40 or more gallons onto the floor.

A leak from the tank body itself cannot be patched. If water comes from the bottom of the tank, plan for replacement.

How to find the leak without guessing

Dry the area with a towel and watch where the first drop appears. Check the pipes above the heater and the pressure relief valve first, because those are the cheapest to fix. Look inside the burner access panel, if your heater has one. Rust streaks on the tank, dampness around the base and a rusty puddle all suggest the tank is failing.

A tank that is over ten years old and leaking from the bottom is nearly always at the end of its life. The inside of the tank is steel, and the anode rod that protects it wears out. Once the lining goes, the tank rusts through from the inside. A new anode rod can extend a healthy tank, but it cannot fix a tank that has already started leaking.

Leaks that are repairable

  • Supply line connectionsFlexible connectors wear out. New ones fix the leak in minutes.
  • Pressure relief valveA worn or crusted valve can be replaced in a short visit, once the cause of the drip is known.
  • Drain valveA cheap valve at the bottom can weep. A plumber can swap it.
  • Expansion tankA waterlogged or leaking expansion tank can be replaced.
  • Dielectric nipplesCorroded nipples at the top of the tank are replaceable.

What the City of Milpitas expects when a tank is replaced

If the tank has to go, Milpitas requires a plumbing permit for the replacement. According to the city's guidance, the tank needs two seismic straps, a relief valve piped to a safe discharge, and a seismic gas shutoff valve where gas is used. A thermal expansion tank is required where the system is closed.

If your garage heater is on the floor and the tank is leaking, the replacement may bring other code upgrades with it. Asking early prevents a failed inspection. See water heater replacement for how we handle the permit. For a house near the San Jose line, our San Jose service page explains the San Jose permit route.

Repair or replace: the decision

If the heater is under eight years old and the leak is at a fitting or valve, repair is usually the better value. If the heater is ten years or older and water comes from the tank, replacement is the sensible choice. Between those ages, look at the whole picture, including rust in the hot water, rumbling noises and how often you have called for repairs.

A tank can fail slowly or all at once. A small leak can become a flood overnight. If you are unsure, shut the water off and have the heater inspected. We tell you plainly whether a repair makes sense, with no pressure to replace.

If the heater is borderline, we show you the rust, the sediment and the age on the data plate, so the decision rests on what you can see.

How to prevent the next leak

  • Flush the tank once a yearIt clears sediment that traps heat at the bottom and wears the lining.
  • Check the anode rod every few yearsIt is the sacrificial part that protects the steel. Replace it when it is mostly gone.
  • Install a drain pan with a drain lineA pan catches a leak and sends it somewhere safe.
  • Keep pressure under 80 psiHigh pressure stresses the tank and every fitting.
  • Add an expansion tank in a closed systemIt absorbs the pressure spike that comes with each heating cycle.
  • Replace flexible connectors that look corrodedThey are cheap and often the first to fail.

What a plumber does on a leak call

  1. Locate the leak

    Dry and trace each fitting and the tank body.

  2. Test the pressure

    A gauge on a hose bib shows incoming pressure, which affects the valve.

  3. Explain the options

    Repair, replace or monitor, with prices.

  4. Do the work

    Parts are swapped or the tank is replaced to code.

  5. Test and clean up

    Check every joint, test the valve and tidy the area.
