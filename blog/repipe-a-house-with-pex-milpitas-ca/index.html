How to Repipe a House with PEX: Milpitas, CA Homeowner Guide

PEX is flexible plastic tubing that has become the most common choice for whole-house repiping. It is fast to install, resists corrosion and handles movement well. A repipe is still a major project with permit steps, and a few details decide whether the result lasts. This guide explains the process and the Milpitas rules in plain terms.

Why homeowners choose PEX

PEX tubing bends around corners, so it needs fewer joints than rigid pipe. Fewer joints mean fewer places to leak. It does not corrode, and it tolerates a degree of freezing better than copper. The installation is quicker than soldering copper, which usually means lower labor.

Copper is still a good pipe, but it can pit in some water and its cost is higher. Galvanized steel, found in the oldest houses, corrodes from the inside and slowly chokes the flow. If your faucets sputter, your water has a rusty tint or you have had several pinhole leaks, repiping may be cheaper than patching. See our repiping page for how we assess a house.

PEX types in plain words

TypeHow it is madeNotes
PEX-ACross-linked by peroxide during extrusionMost flexible, expansion fittings, can recover from kinks.
PEX-BCross-linked with silane after extrusionStiffer, crimp or clamp rings, widely available.
PEX-CCross-linked by radiationLess common, similar to B in use.

Whichever type is used, the City of Milpitas expects the product to be third-party listed and certified to NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 for drinking water.

How a PEX repipe is done

  1. Map the house

    Count fixtures, trace existing lines and choose a layout. Two common layouts are trunk-and-branch and home-run manifold.

  2. Pull the permit

    A plumbing permit is requested before the work begins.

  3. Shut off and drain

    The main valve is closed and the old lines are drained.

  4. Run the new lines

    PEX is pulled through the attic, crawlspace or walls to each fixture. Drywall is opened only where needed.

  5. Make the connections

    Fittings are crimped or expanded. Dielectric unions join PEX to copper or steel where metals meet.

  6. Test the system

    The lines are pressurized and held for at least 15 minutes per the city handout.

  7. Rough inspection

    The inspector sees the pipes before walls are closed.

  8. Finish and final inspection

    Drywall is patched, fixtures are reconnected and the final inspection follows.

What the Milpitas handout requires for PEX

  • Support spacingPEX 1 inch and smaller needs support every 32 inches along horizontal runs.
  • Protection near framingPipe within 1 inch of the edge of a stud or plate needs a No. 18 gauge steel nail plate.
  • Sleeves in soilPEX buried outdoors needs a protective sleeve.
  • SunlightPEX must be shielded from UV light, because sunlight weakens it.
  • InsulationHot water piping requires insulation.
  • DisinfectionNew potable systems must be disinfected before use.

Can you do a PEX repipe yourself

A handy person can handle part of it. PEX tools are simple, and kits are sold at hardware stores. The challenge is planning, since a whole-house repipe covers every fixture. It also needs a permit and two inspections, and the inspector expects tidy, supported runs that follow the code.

Many homeowners do their own demolition and let the plumber handle the pipe. Others hire the whole job to avoid being without water for days. The city says the permit may be issued to the homeowner or to a licensed contractor, and that a contractor must complete work if a homeowner fails an inspection.

That is useful to know before you start. If you want us to handle the whole job, call and we schedule an assessment. We look at pipe age, pressure and wall access first, then explain the layout, the permit steps and the days you should expect to be without water.

Local note

Why repiping comes up in Milpitas

Milpitas incorporated in 1954 and built out through the 1960s, 70s and 80s. Homes from those decades often have copper that is now 40 to 60 years old, and some have galvanized or older polybutylene lines. Pinhole leaks, drops in pressure and tinted water are the usual signs.

The City of Milpitas requires a plumbing permit to install or replace water piping, and a building permit if extensive wall or ceiling work is involved. Repairs that do not replace pipe are exempt. A repipe is always new work.

For homes in North San Jose, the City of San Jose also requires a plumbing permit for a repipe, plus a building permit if shear walls or structural members are affected, according to the city.

Before you decide

Ask whether a partial repipe makes sense. If only one bathroom or one wall has failing pipe, a partial job may solve the problem for less. Ask the plumber what warranty applies to the pipe and the labor, and ask for it in writing. And ask about the pressure regulator, since a repipe is the right time to replace a failing one.

If a pipe has burst already, start with burst pipe repair and plan the repipe after the water is stopped.
