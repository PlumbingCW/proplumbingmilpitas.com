Whole-House Water Filtration: Milpitas, CA Homeowner Guide

A main water filtration system treats the water where it enters the house, so every tap and appliance gets filtered water. It sounds simple, but filtration is a family of different products that do different jobs. Choosing the wrong one wastes money. This guide explains what each does and how to start with the problem you actually have.

Start with the problem, not the product

People install filters for four reasons: taste and smell, sediment and cloudiness, hard water scale, or a concern about a specific contaminant. Each reason points to a different product. A carbon filter helps with chlorine taste and odor. A sediment filter catches sand and particles. A softener handles scale. Reverse osmosis is for drinking water at a single tap.

Before buying anything, get the facts. The City of Milpitas publishes an annual water quality report, also called a consumer confidence report, that lists what is in the water supplied to your home. Read it first. If a test of your own tap shows something unusual, a certified lab can run a more detailed test.

Filter types and what each does

TypeRemoves or reducesWhere it fits
Sediment filterSand, silt, rust flakesWhole house, first stage.
Carbon block or granularChlorine taste, odor, some organicsWhole house or one tap.
Salt-based softenerCalcium and magnesium (hardness)Whole house.
Reverse osmosisDissolved solids and many contaminantsUnder-sink, drinking tap.
UV lightBacteria and virusesPrivate wells, not city water.
Scale conditionerReduces scale adhesion without removing mineralsWhole house, light scale.

No single filter handles everything. Combine stages only for reasons you can name.

What a whole-house filter cannot do

It does not make city water into pure water. It does not remove everything, and it does not fix a pressure problem. A carbon filter can reduce pressure if it is undersized or clogged, and filters need cartridge changes on schedule. A neglected filter can harbor bacteria, so replacement is part of the cost.

It also does not replace a softener. Filters take things out of the water, but they leave the hardness minerals alone. If your issue is white scale, you want a softener or a scale conditioner. See water softener and filtration for how we match a system to a problem.

How a whole-house system is installed

  1. Pick the location

    The system goes on the main line after the shutoff and before the water heater, usually in the garage.

  2. Shut off the water

    The main is closed and the line is drained.

  3. Cut in the system

    Unions and a bypass valve are installed so you can service the filter later.

  4. Install the pressure gauge

    Gauges before and after show when the filter needs changing.

  5. Flush and start

    The cartridge is flushed and the line is tested for leaks.

  6. Set the service schedule

    Cartridge change dates are noted for you.

Local note

What the City of Milpitas publishes about its water

The City of Milpitas publishes the hardness and the sources of its supply in that report. Hardness in recent reports ranges from about 8.4 to 168 ppm, with averages around 57 to 110 ppm depending on the supply. The city also lists its treatment disinfectant and monitoring results in the report.

Because the mix of sources changes through the year, the same tap can taste different in different months. A filter with a carbon stage smooths out those changes. Ask the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety whether your installation needs a plumbing permit before work begins.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What problem does this solveIf the seller cannot say, keep looking.
  • What is the flow ratingMatch it to the number of bathrooms so pressure stays steady.
  • How often do cartridges changeAsk for the price of a replacement set.
  • Is it certifiedLook for NSF/ANSI certification for the claims made.
  • Is there a bypassYou need one for service.
  • Who installs and services itParts and labor should be available locally.

A simple maintenance schedule

PartCheck or changeWhy it matters
Sediment cartridgeEvery 3 to 6 months, sooner if the gauge shows a pressure dropA clogged cartridge starves the house of flow.
Carbon cartridgeEvery 6 to 12 monthsSpent carbon stops removing taste and odor.
Housing O-ringAt every cartridge changeA dry or flat O-ring leaks.
Pressure gaugesGlance at them monthlyA widening gap between the two gauges shows clogging.
Bypass valveOperate it once a yearA seized bypass is useless in an emergency.

Write the change dates on the housing with a marker, so the schedule does not depend on memory.

A simple plan for most Milpitas homes

For a typical house, start with a sediment prefilter and a carbon filter for taste if chlorine bothers you. Add a softener if scale is an issue. Add a reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen sink only if you want the extra reduction for drinking water. Skip the rest unless a test points to a specific need.
