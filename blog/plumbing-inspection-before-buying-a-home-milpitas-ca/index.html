Plumbing Inspection Before Buying a Home: Milpitas, CA Buyer Guide

A general home inspection looks at plumbing, but only at what is visible. A separate plumbing inspection goes further, and a camera look at the sewer line goes furthest. If you are buying a home in Milpitas, spending a little before closing can save you from a repair bill after. This guide shows what to check and what each level of inspection finds.

Three levels of plumbing inspection

LevelWhat is checkedWhat it can miss
General home inspectionVisible pipes, fixtures, water heater, main shutoff and running waterBuried and in-wall pipe, sewer line condition.
Full plumbing inspectionPressure test, fixtures, valves, drains, water heater, gas lines and visible supply and waste pipesHidden leaks behind walls, the buried sewer lateral.
Plumbing inspection with sewer cameraAll of the above plus the inside of the sewer lateralLeaks in slab or concealed supply lines.

For a house over 30 years old, the sewer camera is the one extra most buyers wish they had added. See sewer camera inspection.

What a plumber checks inside the house

The inspector tests water pressure with a gauge and checks that hot and cold run at every fixture. They look for leaks under sinks and around toilets, inspect the water heater for age, strapping and a discharge pipe, and check the main shutoff. Pipe material is noted. Galvanized steel, polybutylene and old copper each mean different future risks.

The inspector also runs several fixtures at once to see whether the drain keeps up. Slow drains, gurgling traps and stains on ceilings under bathrooms are all clues. If a problem is found, a plumber can tell you whether it is a minor repair or the start of a bigger project.

Red flags in a Milpitas house

  • Galvanized or polybutylene supply linesBoth are known for failing. A repipe may be coming. See repiping.
  • Water heater older than 10 to 12 yearsBudget for replacement. See water heater replacement.
  • No seismic straps or gas shutoff on the heaterThese are expected in this area and are required in new work.
  • Low water pressure at several fixturesIt may signal a failing service line or corroded pipe.
  • Stains on ceilings or under sinksSigns of old or current leaks.
  • Slow drains in several fixturesOften a sign of a main line problem.
  • Unpermitted additions with plumbingA home with an added bathroom but no permit record can cause trouble.

Why the sewer lateral deserves a camera

The sewer lateral is the pipe that carries waste from your house to the city main. In older neighborhoods it is often clay or cast iron, and it can crack, shift or fill with roots. A damaged lateral may cost a great deal to fix, and the problem is invisible until it fails.

A camera inspection runs a small camera down the line and records what it sees. You get a video and a written finding. If you are in the middle of a real estate contract, a camera inspection result gives you something specific to negotiate. See also sewer line repair to understand the fix options before you commit.

Local note

What a Milpitas buyer should know about permits and records

The City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety issues plumbing permits for water heater replacements, repipes and new fixtures. An unpermitted water heater swap, a bathroom addition or a repipe done without a permit is a risk for the next owner. The city says repairs that do not replace or rearrange pipes are exempt, so a simple leak fix is fine, but a replacement is permit work.

Ask the seller whether permits exist for major plumbing work, and check with the city if the answer is unclear. The same advice applies for a house in North San Jose, where the City of San Jose notes that insurers may deny claims related to unpermitted work. our San Jose service page summarizes how that city handles it.

Source type: City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety and City of San Jose Permit Center.

How to order the inspection

  1. Add the plumbing inspection to your contingency period

    Do it within the deadline in your contract.

  2. Ask for the sewer camera add-on

    Request a recording and a written report.

  3. Attend the inspection if you can

    Seeing the shutoff and the water heater helps.

  4. Review the written findings

    Rank repairs into urgent, soon and watch.

  5. Use the report in negotiation

    Ask for repairs, a credit or a price change.

Questions to ask the inspector

  • What is the water pressure at the hose bibA number above 80 psi means a regulator may be failing.
  • How old is the water heaterThe data plate shows the build date in the serial number.
  • What is the supply pipe made ofCopper, PEX, galvanized and older plastic each carry different risks.
  • Are there signs of past leaksStained cabinets, patched drywall and fresh paint in odd places are clues.
  • Did you run the drains togetherSeveral fixtures at once show whether the drain keeps up.
  • Where are the cleanout and the main shutoffYou should know both before you move in.

After you close

Locate the main water shutoff and show every household member. Photograph the water heater label and note its age. Put the plumber's phone number where everyone can find it. If the inspection flagged a small leak or an old valve, schedule that repair early rather than waiting for it to fail.
