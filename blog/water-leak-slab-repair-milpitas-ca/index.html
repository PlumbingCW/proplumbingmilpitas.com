Slab Leak Next Steps: Confirming, Documenting and Claiming in Milpitas, CA

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe beneath a concrete floor. The signs are subtle and the fix can be disruptive, so the early steps matter. This guide is about the first days: confirming that you have one, documenting it for your insurer and weighing the repair routes. For the repair methods themselves, see our slab leak repair page.

Step 1: confirm it is a slab leak

  1. Check the meter

    Turn off all water and look at the meter. If the dial moves, water is escaping.

  2. Shut the main and check again

    If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the buried line.

  3. Look for signs

    Warm floor spots, damp edges on carpet and the sound of running water point to a slab leak.

  4. Check the water heater side

    A leak on a hot line may make the heater run constantly.

  5. Call for detection

    A plumber uses listening gear and thermal imaging to mark the spot. See leak detection.

Why slab leaks happen

Pipes under a slab can fail from corrosion, from abrasion against the concrete or gravel, from high water pressure and from ground movement. Copper pipe in direct contact with abrasive gravel can wear through over decades. Soil movement and earthquakes can also stress lines.

High pressure speeds the damage. A pressure regulator that is failing can send more than 80 psi into the lines, which stresses every joint. A pressure check is part of any good leak diagnosis, and the fix may be as simple as replacing the regulator along with the failed pipe.

Step 2: document for your insurer

  • Photos and videoCapture stains, warped flooring and the meter reading before cleanup.
  • Meter readings with datesWrite down a reading before and after a test period.
  • Water billsKeep recent bills that show the increase.
  • Written detection reportAsk the plumber for a dated report with the leak location.
  • ReceiptsKeep every receipt for detection, repair and drying.
  • Time of discoveryNote when you first noticed signs.

What insurance typically covers and what it does not

Home policies differ, so read yours or ask your agent. Many cover the damage from a sudden and accidental leak, such as ruined flooring or drywall. Many exclude the cost of repairing the pipe itself, and some exclude damage from a slow leak that went on for a long time. Some cover tearing out and replacing the surface that was opened to reach the leak.

Because policies vary so much, call your insurer before you start any work and ask what documentation they want. A claim may also require an adjuster to see the damage before cleanup. We provide dated reports and photos that support a claim, but we cannot say what a given policy will pay.

Local note

Permits and records in Milpitas

The City of Milpitas says repairs which do not replace or rearrange pipes are exempt from a plumbing permit. Replacing or installing water piping, as in a reroute or a repipe, requires a plumbing permit and typically two inspections. Extensive removal of walls or ceilings can also need a building permit.

Keep the permit record with your house papers, because a future buyer or insurer may ask for it. Homes built in the 1950s through the 1980s on slabs are where we see most slab leaks in this area.

If you are in North San Jose, the City of San Jose lists a plumbing permit requirement for repipes and water service replacement, and our San Jose service page summarizes how we handle that side.

Questions to ask a plumber about a slab leak

  • How did you confirm the locationLook for a method, not just a guess. Pressure tests, listening gear and thermal imaging are standard.
  • What are my options and what does each costSpot repair, reroute and repipe differ in price and disruption.
  • Who handles the permitA reroute or repipe needs one.
  • What happens to the floorAsk who restores tile, wood or carpet.
  • Will you give a written reportYou need it for an insurance claim.
  • What does the warranty coverAsk about both labor and parts, and for how long.

Why speed matters after you suspect a slab leak

A slab leak runs day and night, so the cost of waiting is measured in wasted water and in damage that spreads sideways under the floor. Moisture can lift tile, swell wood flooring and feed mold in the walls above. Detection takes hours, not days, and a clear location lets the repair stay small.

If you cannot get a plumber right away, shutting the main valve at night and opening it only for daytime use is a reasonable stopgap. Tell the plumber you are doing it, and keep the meter readings you take.

What not to do

Do not drill or break the slab yourself. Water can travel a long way under concrete, so the wet spot is often not above the leak. Do not ignore the signs either. A slab leak runs all day and all night and can soften the soil beneath the slab.
