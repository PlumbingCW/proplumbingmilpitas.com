When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most slow drains can wait for a daytime visit. A drain turns urgent when wastewater has nowhere to go, because it will find the lowest opening in the house.

Stop using water

Every flush, shower and dishwasher cycle sends more water toward the blockage. Pause all of it until a plumber has looked.

Protect the floor

Towels at the base of a tub or toilet buy time. Move rugs and cardboard boxes away from a floor drain.

Leave the chemicals on the shelf

Caustic drain openers sit in a standing blockage and can burn skin or damage the pipe. They also make a plumber's job more hazardous.

Find the cleanout

A capped pipe near the house foundation or along the side yard is the cleanout. Knowing where it is saves time when we arrive.

The drain that is blocked tells us where to look

One slow fixture usually means a blockage close to that fixture, such as hair in a tub trap or grease in a kitchen branch line. Two or three fixtures slowing at once points farther down the line, and a floor drain that gurgles when the washer drains points to the main.

The lowest drain in the house, often a shower or a laundry standpipe, is the first to back up when the main sewer line is partly blocked. If water from the washer rises in the tub, the stoppage is past every branch line, and a sewer line cleaning visit is the right fix.

Kitchen sink clogs in Milpitas homes

The kitchen sink is the most common place for a stubborn blockage, and the cause is rarely the last thing that went down the drain.

  • Grease that cooled in the lineFat leaves the sink as a liquid and hardens a few feet later, where it collects food particles and builds a plug over months.
  • A garbage disposal that cannot clearFibrous peels and coffee grounds pack the trap arm behind the disposal. A garbage disposal repair visit is sometimes part of the fix.
  • A double sink with a shared trapWhen both basins back up together, the blockage is in the shared drain, not in either basin's strainer.
  • A dishwasher that drains into the same lineStanding water in the dishwasher after a cycle often means the shared branch is restricted.

Who owns which part of the sewer in Milpitas

Milpitas staff maintain the public sanitary sewer system, but the pipe between your house and the city main is split in two. The city says the user must clear stoppages along the house lateral, and that the property owner is responsible for the private house sewer from the property line to the building.

If a stoppage came from grease, rags or other material from your property, or if the city finds no stoppage at all, the city's sewer page says the user pays the city's costs. Calling a plumber first avoids that bill. If the plumber finds the pipe broken in the public portion, the city says it makes those repairs at no charge, so tell the plumber to document the camera footage.

During business hours, Milpitas Public Works takes sewer calls at (408) 586-2600. After hours and on holidays the city lists police dispatch at (408) 586-2400.

What to do first, and the code behind it

Before a cable machine or jet touches a line, we confirm where the cleanout is, because the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) expects drains to have access for cleaning. According to the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety, clearing a blockage is a repair that needs no permit, while replacing a section of pipe does.
