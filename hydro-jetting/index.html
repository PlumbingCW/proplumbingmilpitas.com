What hydro jetting does that a snake cannot

A snake punches a hole through a blockage and pulls some of it out. The pipe wall behind that hole stays coated, and the next load of grease sticks to what is left. Hydro jetting sends water from a hose with a rotating nozzle at the pipe, and the spray cuts the coating off the wall and flushes it to the city main.

The nozzle has jets that pull it forward and others that spray back toward the operator. That is how the hose travels up to 100 feet or more through a 4 inch line without being pushed by hand.

Signs your line needs jetting, not another snaking

The same drain clogs twice in a year

A repeat clog means debris is being left behind on the wall, not cleared.

Slow drains in every bathroom

One slow fixture is a branch problem. Every fixture slowing together points to the main.

Gurgling toilets after laundry

Air is being pushed back through traps because the main cannot take the washer's discharge.

Kitchen line backs up after cooking

Years of fat on the pipe wall are the usual cause in older kitchens.

Why a camera comes first

Jetting is hard on a weak pipe. Before we open the water, we run a sewer camera to look at the pipe, because water pressure should not go into a line that is cracked, offset at a joint or collapsed. The camera also shows whether roots are the problem. If they are, a cutting head clears the roots first and jetting finishes the job.

This is why a sewer inspection and jetting are often booked together. The camera footage tells you what was in the line and whether a sewer line repair is the better next step.

Standards behind the work

Jetting is a cleaning method, so no permit applies, but the pipe it cleans is governed by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). According to the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety, replacing or rearranging drain pipe is permit work, which is why we camera the line first and tell you if a repair is needed.
