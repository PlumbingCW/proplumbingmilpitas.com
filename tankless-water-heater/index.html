What a tankless installation involves

A tankless heater needs a lot of gas for a short time. The City of Milpitas handout notes that replacing a gas storage heater with a gas tankless unit may require upsizing the gas piping to meet the tankless unit's minimum input rating. That is the most common surprise in a retrofit.

Venting is the next question. Tankless units use special sealed venting, and the city says plastic venting may be used only when the appliance is listed for it. We set the vent per the manufacturer and the California Plumbing Code.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Scale from hard water builds on the heat exchanger and shortens the unit's life. The City of Milpitas 2024 water quality report lists average total hardness of roughly 57 to 110 ppm for its two supplies, about 3 to 6.5 grains per gallon, with readings as high as 168 ppm.

  • Moderate hardness still scalesWater at 6 grains per gallon leaves a hard coating over time, especially when the unit runs at high temperature.
  • The flushWe isolate the unit, connect a pump and circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, then rinse the system.
  • Follow the manufacturerEach brand states its own interval, and some require flushing to keep the warranty valid.
  • A softener helpsIf your supply tests harder than average or you have an older unit, see water softener and filtration.

If you are switching from a tank

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District says the zero-NOx rule for residential water heaters applies when a unit is replaced, with the compliance date moved to January 1, 2028 by its 2026 amendments. Gas tankless units draw a lot of gas, so ask about that rule before you buy.

A tank that has failed overnight needs water heater replacement first. A planned swap can be handled as a water heater installation with time to compare models.
