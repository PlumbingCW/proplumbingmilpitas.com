The Great Mall of the Bay Area and the streets around it form a major retail district in Milpitas. The City covers it, along with the Milpitas BART station area, in its Milpitas Metro Specific Plan. This is a commercial and mixed-use district, not a single-family neighborhood.

A district built on the old Ford plant site

The Great Mall opened in 1994 on the site of the former Ford assembly plant, which closed in 1984. The City Housing Element describes the Great Mall site as about 100 acres and plans to rezone it from general commercial to mixed use.

The Milpitas Metro Specific Plan was adopted on February 7, 2023. It covers about 510 gross acres and updates the 2008 Transit Area Specific Plan, which covered about 437 acres. We do not know the timing of any project, so ask your landlord what work is planned for your building.

Plumbing needs for retail, restaurants and offices

  • Restaurants and food serviceGrease builds up in drain lines and backs them up. Regular hydro jetting clears the buildup. Ask your landlord and the City whether your space needs a grease interceptor.
  • Public restroomsToilets, faucets and flush valves see heavy use. See commercial plumbing.
  • Water heaters for a tenant spaceReplacing one usually needs the landlord to approve it. See water heater replacement.
  • Apartments and condos nearbyStart with your property manager or HOA, then have a plumber find the cause. See leak detection.

Tenant, landlord and the lease

In a multi-tenant building, the lease decides who pays for a repair. A drain that serves several units is usually the owner's concern, and a fixture inside your space is usually yours, but the wording varies. Read the lease before you order work.

When you call, tell us whether you are the owner or a tenant and whether the line serves one space or several. That tells the plumber who must approve the job and who gets the written findings.
