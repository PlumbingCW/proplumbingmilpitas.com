City water line work and your own plumbing

The Housing Element lists a Dempsey Road Water Line project in its Capital Improvement Program under North Park Victoria, and it lists Dempsey Road resurfacing between South Park Victoria and Yosemite Drive. We do not know the schedule or status of either project. The City of Milpitas Public Works department can tell you.

City main work is a City job. It is separate from the pipes inside your home. It can still cause short shutoffs or pressure changes, and the steps below protect your fixtures when the water comes back.

After a water shutoff or pressure change

  1. Open a cold tap and let it run

    Start with the lowest tap in the house and then a higher one. Air and sediment clear out in a few minutes, and the water runs clear.

  2. Rinse the faucet aerators

    Unscrew the aerator at the end of each faucet and rinse out grit. A clogged aerator is a common cause of weak flow after a shutoff.

  3. Check for drips

    Look under sinks, behind toilets and around the water heater once pressure is back. A small drip that was masked at low pressure can show up now.

  4. Call if flow stays low or you see water

    If flow stays weak at every tap, or water is wet anywhere it should not be, call and describe it. See leak detection and water heater repair.

Local note

Chloramine in the City water

The City of Milpitas disinfects its water with chloramine, according to its 2024 water quality report. If you keep a fish tank or a pond, ask a pet store how to condition the water, because chloramine is treated differently than chlorine. This is general information and not a claim about any one home.

Source type: City of Milpitas 2024 Water Quality Report.
