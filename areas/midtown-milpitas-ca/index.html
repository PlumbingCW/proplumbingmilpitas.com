Plumber in Midtown, Milpitas, CA

Midtown is the part of Milpitas around Main Street and Abel Street, and it is described as the oldest part of the city. The City of Milpitas now calls the area the Gateway-Main Street Specific Plan area, after the Midtown Specific Plan adopted in 2002 and updated in 2010.

Older buildings and new mixed-use projects share these blocks. This page explains how that mix affects plumbing calls, who decides when a pipe is shared, and who to call first. Call (510) 544-1962 to talk it through.

An old core that is still changing

The City says many sites along Main Street are vacant or underused and that about 1,200 housing units in the plan area have been approved or built. It also points to reinvestment at the Great Mall and a light rail extension nearby. The City began updating the plan in the fall of 2021.

For plumbing, that means one block can hold a building from the early days of the city beside a newer apartment or condo building. Each has its own water service, shutoffs and drains, so the first step is always to find out which building and which pipe you are dealing with.

What plumbing needs fit a mixed old and new area

  • Houses and small older buildingsRepeating drain backups, worn fixtures and tired water heaters. See drain cleaning and water heater repair.
  • Condos, townhomes and apartmentsWater on a ceiling or wall may come from your unit or from a shared line. See leak detection to find the source.
  • Small shops and restaurants near Main StreetRestroom fixtures, water heaters and floor drains. See commercial plumbing.
  • Remodels and additionsMoving a sink or adding a bathroom needs a plan and usually a permit. See plumbing installation.

Shared pipes in a multi-unit building

In a condo, the association documents decide which pipes belong to the association and which belong to you. In a rental, the landlord decides. Ask before you pay for repairs to a line that may be shared.

A plumber can find the cause of the leak and tell you what they see. That helps when you report it to the HOA or landlord, and it can stop a dispute about whose pipe failed.

Local note

Check which water source serves your address

The City of Milpitas buys water from the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission and from Valley Water. Most of the water comes through the SFPUC, and parts of the city west of Interstate 880 and south of State Route 237 are served by Valley Water. The City publishes a water supply map that shows which source reaches an address.

The source matters when you pick a softener or filter, because the two supplies are treated differently before the City distributes them. Tell the plumber which source your address gets.
