Toilet overflowing: stop it in under a minute

Do these in order.

Lift the tank lid

Push the flapper down into its seat to stop water entering the bowl.

Close the supply valve

Turn the oval handle behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

Do not flush again

Another flush on a blocked toilet sends more water to the floor.

Call us

If the water keeps rising after the valve is shut, the problem is a main line blockage. See drain cleaning.

Repair or replace: how we decide

A toilet is worth repairing when the porcelain is sound and the parts are standard. A flapper, fill valve or wax ring is a short job and the toilet lasts years longer. Replacement makes sense when the bowl or tank is cracked, when the toilet is an older high-volume model that still clogs, or when repeated repairs add up.

California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush through its plumbing code, so a modern replacement uses far less water than a pre-1990s model. We tell you what we see and let you choose.

How a toilet installation goes

  1. Shut off and drain

    We close the supply, empty the tank and bowl and disconnect the supply line.

  2. Pull and inspect

    The old toilet comes up and we check the flange and the floor for rot or damage.

  3. Set the new unit

    A new wax ring or seal, new bolts and a level fit.

  4. Test

    We flush several times, check every joint for seepage and caulk the base if you want it.

When the toilet is not the problem

If several fixtures drain slowly, if a tub gurgles when the toilet flushes or if the toilet backs up when the washer runs, the blockage is in the branch or main drain. A new toilet will not fix that. We check before replacing anything, because a camera look costs less than the wrong replacement. See sewer camera inspection.

A toilet that leaks at the base for a long time can rot the subfloor. If we find soft flooring we show you before covering it.

Standards behind the work

The 1.28 gallon per flush limit for new toilets comes from California plumbing rules built on the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). According to the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety, repairs that do not replace pipe need no permit.
