What a sewer camera inspection shows

The camera runs from a cleanout or a pulled toilet to the street connection and sends live video to a monitor. We record it so you can watch it again and share it with an agent, an insurer or the city.

A good run shows pipe material and diameter, joints that have shifted, cracks, roots, scale, a belly where water pools and any point where the pipe has collapsed. A locating tool at the camera head marks the surface above each defect so repairs can be planned without guesswork.

How the visit works

  1. Find the access

    We locate your cleanout or pull a fixture if there is none.

  2. Clear what blocks the view

    A light cleaning pass may be needed so the lens can see the wall.

  3. Run and record

    The camera goes to the street connection while we note footage at each defect.

  4. Mark and explain

    You get the video, the location of each problem and a plain next step.

When a camera inspection is worth booking

  • Before buying an older homeHomes that predate the 1980s may have clay or cast iron laterals. A camera costs far less than discovering a collapsed line after closing.
  • After a repeat clogTwo backups in a year point to something wrong in the pipe, not in the drain.
  • Before a landscaping or concrete projectKnowing where the lateral runs and how deep it sits avoids damaging it.
  • When the city asks questionsIf a sewer problem touches the public side, the video shows where the pipe fails.

Why Milpitas inspections matter for permits

The city says an underground inspection for a sewer line replacement includes a camera check and that the contractor must have a camera on site when the inspector arrives. A video from before the job helps you plan the work and compare bids.

If your property sits in the Sunnyhills area or another neighborhood built after the city incorporated in 1954, the original lateral may still be in the ground. A camera tells you whether it is still sound.

Standards behind the inspection

A camera inspection is observation only, so no permit is needed. What we find is judged against the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and against the permit guidance published by the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety.
