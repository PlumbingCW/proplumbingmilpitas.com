Why a drip is worth fixing

One drip per second adds up to thousands of gallons a year. The waste is real and the sound is annoying, and in a house with hard water the drip leaves mineral stains in the sink. Milpitas water ranges from soft to fairly hard depending on the supply source, with city reports showing averages around 57 to 110 ppm and a wide range over the year.

Minerals clog aerators and age cartridges faster in the harder periods.

Fixing a faucet early is a small job. Waiting until the handle will not turn can turn it into a replacement.

Types of faucets we work on

  • Single-handle kitchen faucetsCartridge or ceramic disc designs, common in newer kitchens.
  • Two-handle bath faucetsWasher-type and cartridge-type, both repairable.
  • Pull-down and pull-out sprayersHose and weight problems, diverter failures and leaks at the spray head.
  • Shower and tub valvesDripping tub spouts and shower valves where the cartridge has failed.
  • Outdoor hose bibsDripping or leaking spigots. A burst hose bib line is covered under burst pipe repair.
  • Bar and laundry sinksSmaller faucets with the same parts as larger ones.

Repair or replace the faucet

If the faucet body is sound and parts are available, repair is the cheaper path. Older faucets from discontinued lines can be hard to source, and a corroded body will not seal even with new parts. When a replacement makes sense, we can install the one you picked or bring one.

We install new faucets with new supply lines and check the stop valves at the same time, because an old stop valve often leaks the day you disturb it. Replacing a stop valve is a common companion job. For a larger kitchen or bath update, see plumbing installation.

Standards behind the work

Faucet and supply parts must be lead-free and certified to NSF/ANSI/CAN 61, according to the City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety, which follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
