Slab leak: what to do first

A slab leak is urgent when water appears at the floor, when a warm spot develops or when the meter spins quickly.

Close the main shutoff

This stops the leak immediately. Hot water lines may take a moment to drain.

Do not drill or break the floor

Water under a slab can follow a path away from the leak, so the wet spot may not be above it.

Check the water heater

A slab leak on a hot line may make the heater run constantly. Turn it off if the main is closed.

Note the signs

Warm floor, damp carpet edges, a sound of running water and a high bill all help us find it.

Signs of a slab leak

A warm or hot spot on the floor

Hot water line leaking beneath the slab heats the concrete.

Water sound with nothing running

A hiss or trickle inside the floor or wall.

Damp flooring or lifting tile

Moisture rising through grout lines or under carpet.

Unexplained jump in the water bill

Slab leaks run constantly and show on the meter.

Low hot water pressure

A drop in hot flow at fixtures far from the heater.

Cracks in walls or floor

Long-term leaks can soften soil and shift the slab.

How we pinpoint the leak before cutting anything

A slab leak is not always under the wet spot, so cutting there often finds nothing. We close the main and run a pressure test to confirm a supply leak, then use acoustic listening, thermal imaging and a line locator to mark the point. If more than one line runs near the leak, we isolate each.

This is a diagnostic job. For general leak detection on pipes you can reach, see leak detection. We only open the slab at a point we have confirmed.

What the City of Milpitas requires for the repair

The city says replacing or installing water piping within a building needs a plumbing permit, and that extensive removal of walls or ceilings needs a building permit. Rerouted piping must follow the California Plumbing Code table for materials, and the city expects a pressure test held for at least 15 minutes with rough and final inspections.

Leak repairs that do not replace or rearrange pipes are exempt, according to the city's Building Safety FAQ, but a reroute or a repipe is new work. We tell you which side of the line your job falls on.
