When repiping makes more sense than another repair

One leak in 20 years is a repair. Three leaks in three years is a pattern. Pinholes in copper, rust-brown water from galvanized pipe and falling pressure are signs the metal is failing from the inside, and each patch only buys time.

A repipe also suits a remodel where walls are already open, because the labor is cheaper while the studs are exposed. We tell you honestly if your pipe still has years left. For a single failure, see burst pipe repair.

How a whole-house repipe goes

  1. Assess

    We trace the existing lines, check pressure and count fixtures.

  2. Permit and plan

    The plumbing permit is requested. A building permit may also apply when walls are cut extensively.

  3. Rough-in

    New pipes go in through the attic, crawlspace and walls. A rough inspection happens before walls are closed.

  4. Connect and test

    Fixtures are reconnected and the system is pressure tested for at least 15 minutes. Final inspection follows.

What the Milpitas Office of Building Safety expects

  • A plumbing permitInstalling or replacing water piping in a building requires one. Extensive removal of walls or ceiling structure requires a building permit.
  • Approved materialsPipe, fittings, solder and flux must comply with NSF/ANSI/CAN 61, and lead content is limited to 0.25 percent for pipe, fittings and fixtures and 0.2 percent for solder and flux.
  • Dielectric unionsThey are required wherever dissimilar metals connect.
  • Support and protectionPEX 1 inch and smaller is supported every 32 inches horizontally, copper 1 1/2 inch and smaller every 6 feet. Pipe passing within 1 inch of exposed framing needs a No. 18 gauge steel nail plate.
  • Insulation and disinfectionHot water piping must be insulated, and new potable systems must be disinfected before use.
  • Two inspectionsA rough inspection before wall or ceiling membranes go on and a final after work is complete.

Which Milpitas homes tend to need it

Milpitas grew fast after its 1954 incorporation, and many homes date from the 1950s and 1960s. Older homes may still have galvanized pipe, while later homes often have copper that can pinhole. We look at the pipe in the garage, at the water heater connection and under sinks to tell you which you have.

If you plan a bathroom or kitchen remodel, schedule the repipe before the drywall goes up. Combining it with a plumbing installation job saves wall repairs.
