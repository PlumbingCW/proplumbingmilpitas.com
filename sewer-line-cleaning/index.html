Sewer backup: what to do first

A backup is wastewater, so treat every wet surface as contaminated.

Stop all water use

No flushing, no showers, no laundry. Each gallon adds to the overflow.

Keep people out

Children, pets and anyone with a health condition should stay out of the wet room.

Photograph the damage

Pictures of the backup and the water line help any insurance claim you make later.

Call for the right party

If the sewage is in the street or at a manhole, call the city. If it is in your house, call us.

In Milpitas, the city asks you to report sewer spills, overflows and local flooding right away. Public Works takes those calls at (408) 586-2600 during business hours. After hours, the city lists police dispatch at (408) 586-2400.

City main or your line? A quick way to tell

A blockage in the city's main shows up in several neighboring homes at once, and the manhole in the street may be running. A blockage in your own pipe shows up only at your house.

The test is the cleanout. Pull the cap and look. If the cleanout is full of sewage, the blockage is downstream of it, in the lateral or the main. If the cleanout is dry while the house is backing up, the blockage is between the house and the cleanout, inside your own pipe.

A note on Milpitas rules for cleanouts and permits

Milpitas says that if there is no street cleanout, the user must install one. For a sewer line replacement the city's handout asks for cleanouts at intervals not exceeding 100 feet in straight runs, and it requires a camera on site at the underground inspection.

Clearing a stoppage is exempt from a plumbing permit under the California Plumbing Code, according to the city's Building Safety FAQ. If a trap or drainpipe fails and must be replaced with new material, the city treats it as new work that needs a permit.
