Meter to house: which pipe this page covers

The city says its water service line permit covers replacing the existing service piping between the meter and the building. It is not the permit for adding a brand new service with a new city meter. Pipe inside the house is a different job, covered under repiping.

If you cannot tell whether a leak is on the city side or your side of the meter, call Public Works at (408) 586-2600 during business hours before you dig. After hours the city lists (408) 586-2400.

Signs of a failing service line

Meter runs with the house shut off

The test is to close the main at the house and see if the meter still spins.

Wet or soft ground

Water bubbling from the ground or a patch of green grass in dry season.

Low pressure throughout

A leak or a partly collapsed pipe reduces flow to every fixture.

Discolored water

Old galvanized or corroded pipe flaking inside.

High bill with no change in use

A buried leak runs 24 hours a day.

Sound of running water at the meter

A hiss near the meter or the house wall.

What the city handout requires for a water service line

  • A permitIt may be issued to a licensed contractor with the proper classification or to the homeowner. If the homeowner or their workers fail inspection, a licensed contractor must finish the job.
  • Approved pipeBrass, copper, CPVC, PEX, PE-RT, stainless steel and others, all lead-free and rated NSF 61. The service must be at least 3/4 inch, and copper must be at least Type L.
  • Depth and beddingYard piping must be at least 12 inches below finished grade, on a firm bed, with clean backfill 12 inches above the pipe.
  • Tracer wire and sleevesPlastic service pipe outdoors needs an 18 AWG blue insulated copper tracer wire, and PEX in soil must be sleeved.
  • Shutoff valveA fullway shutoff valve must sit on the discharge side of the meter and stay accessible.
  • Test and inspectThe pipe must hold pressure for at least 15 minutes. A rough inspection happens before the trench is covered and a final follows. Permits expire 180 days after issuance or the last passed inspection.

Low pressure in Milpitas homes is not always the line

Low pressure can come from a stuck pressure regulator, a half-closed valve, clogged fixture screens or a partly failed service line. We test pressure with a gauge at a hose bib to see whether the problem is the supply or the house.

The California Plumbing Code in the city handout calls for a pressure regulator where pressure exceeds 80 psi. A regulator that fails open pushes pressure above that, and one that fails closed gives you a trickle. A pressure test costs far less than digging the yard.
