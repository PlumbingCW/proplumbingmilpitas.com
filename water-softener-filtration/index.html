How hard is Milpitas water

The City of Milpitas reports its water quality each year. Hardness reported in the city's consumer confidence report ranges from about 8.4 to 168 ppm, with averages around 57 to 110 ppm depending on the supply. That is roughly 3 to 6 grains per gallon on average, and softer than much of the country but enough to leave scale on fixtures over time.

Because the mix of sources changes through the year, your tap can run softer or harder in different months. A test at your own tap gives the figure that matters for your house, and the figure decides whether a softener is worth installing.

Things to know before you install a softener

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration water and an outlet. Some local water districts and cities have rules on salt-based softener discharge, so check with the city before you buy. We can tell you what to ask.

Softened water uses sodium. If anyone in the home follows a low-sodium diet, ask your doctor, and consider leaving the kitchen drinking tap on an unsoftened line. Also tell us if you have a tankless heater, since scale protection matters most there. See tankless water heaters.

Sizing a softener to your household

Capacity depends on how many people live in the home and how hard the water tests. A family of four with moderate hardness needs a smaller unit than a family of six with the harder end of the range. An oversized unit wastes salt and an undersized one regenerates too often.

We measure your tap hardness, ask about the number of bathrooms and check the space and the drain before recommending a size. We also look at the water heater, since scale protection helps most where water is heated.
