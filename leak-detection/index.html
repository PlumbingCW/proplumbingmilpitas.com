When a hidden leak is an emergency

A hidden leak is urgent when water is visibly spreading, when a ceiling is bulging or when a wall is warm or wet to the touch.

Close the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve off at the house. Open one faucet to relieve pressure.

Kill power near wet areas

If water is near outlets, switches or a ceiling light, switch the circuit off at the panel.

Catch what you can

A bucket under a ceiling stain and towels at the base of a wet wall buy time.

Photograph before it dries

Pictures of stains and damage matter for any insurance claim.

Start with the meter: a ten-minute test you can do

Turn off every tap and appliance in the house, including the ice maker. Find your water meter, usually in a box near the street or at the curb, and look at the small dial or triangle that spins at low flow. If it moves with everything off, water is leaving the system somewhere.

Next, close the main valve at the house and check the meter again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps turning, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house. That is the single most useful fact you can give us on the phone.

Where leaks in Milpitas homes tend to hide

  • Ceiling and wall leaksStains below an upstairs bathroom, a sink or a washing machine box. We trace the stain upward and test the supply and drain lines.
  • Under-house and crawlspace leaksHomes with a raised foundation allow access underneath. Damp soil, a musty smell and a spinning meter point here.
  • Buried yard and meter-box leaksThe line between the meter and the house passes through soil, and the shutoff valve in the meter box can leak too. See water line repair and replacement.
  • Under-slab leaksA warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water in a slab home. See slab leak repair.
  • Irrigation linesA sprinkler valve stuck open or a broken head keeps water running for hours. A meter that runs only when the timer runs is a clue.
  • Pool and spa linesIf you have a pool or spa, plumbing to and from the equipment can leak. Mention it on the call.

What a high water bill means and what it does not

A sudden jump in the bill has more than one cause. A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day, an irrigation timer change can raise a summer bill and a billing change can look like a leak. We start with your usage history and then the meter test.

Milpitas Public Works handles city water questions at (408) 586-2600 during business hours, and the city lists (408) 586-2400 after hours. If you suspect the leak is on the city side of the meter, call before you dig. If it is on your side, we find it. A running toilet is a fast fix covered under toilet repair.
