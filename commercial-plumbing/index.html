Permits and inspections for commercial work

The City of Milpitas Office of Building Safety issues plumbing permits for work in commercial buildings as well as homes. A commercial permit is requested by the contractor or the owner, and the scope decides how many inspections are needed. Tenant improvements often add a building permit.

Backflow prevention, grease interceptors and similar devices may also have separate requirements from the water or sewer provider. We tell you which agency to contact for your job rather than guess.

Keeping a business open through a plumbing failure

Most commercial plumbing downtime comes from a few repeat causes: kitchen grease that hardens in the line, paper products in restroom drains and water heaters that fail on a busy day. We ask when it started, how many fixtures are affected and whether a floor drain is backing up, because those answers separate a single clog from a main line problem.

For a restaurant, a blocked floor drain can close the kitchen under health rules, so tell us that on the call and we move it up the schedule. A planned jetting visit every few months is often cheaper than one forced closure. See hydro jetting for how that works.

If you lease, check who owns which pipe. Tenants usually handle fixtures inside the unit and landlords handle shared lines and risers. We write up what we find so both sides can see it, with photos and the location of the failure.
