How a failing sewer line announces itself

Most failing laterals give notice for months. Drains slow, the toilet gurgles when the washer drains, a damp patch appears over the line, or a sewer smell shows up near the cleanout. A single backup can be a clog. Three backups in a year is a pipe problem.

The only way to be sure is a camera run, which shows cracks, offset joints, roots in the joints and bellies where water pools. See sewer inspection for how that works. The footage decides the repair method.

Milpitas Building Safety says trenchless replacement must follow ASTM F1216, that cured-in-place liners shall not be used on collapsed piping, and that cast-iron soil pipe is excluded from the trenchless method its handout describes.

What the city requires for a sewer line job

The city's handout for residential sewer line replacement covers the work between the building drain, 2 feet outside the building, and the public works encroachment line or back of the sidewalk.

  • A permitThe permit may be issued to a state-licensed contractor with the proper classification, to the property owner or to the owner's designee. Work under a sidewalk or street needs an encroachment permit, obtained in person at the Permit Center.
  • Approved pipeThe list includes ABS Schedule 40, PVC Schedule 40, cast iron, copper DWV and stainless steel 316L. All of it must be third-party listed.
  • Slope and sizeSewers need a uniform slope of at least 1/4 inch per foot where practical, and a sewer serving one to three toilets cannot be smaller than 3 inches.
  • TestingThe line is tested water-filled or with a low-pressure air test at 4.0 psi, and plastic DWV is not air-tested.
  • Two inspectionsAn underground inspection with a camera on site, then a final once backfill is complete.

Who owns the repair in Milpitas

The city says it maintains the public sanitary sewer, and that where a pipe in the public lateral section is broken or structurally failed, the City makes the repair at no charge. The private house sewer from the property line to the building belongs to the property owner.

This split makes the camera footage important. If the break is on the public side, the video is what you show the city. If it is on your side, the video is the basis for the repair plan and permit.

Older housing and the likely pipe

Milpitas incorporated in 1954, and the Sunnyhills tract was built for Ford plant workers in the 1950s. Homes from that era often have clay or cast iron laterals, with roots entering at joints. Later homes more often have ABS or PVC.

If you own a home from the first decades after incorporation, assume the lateral has never been replaced until a camera proves otherwise. The camera also tells you whether lining is possible. Roots are a good case for hydro jetting after a spot repair.

What to do first, and the code behind it
